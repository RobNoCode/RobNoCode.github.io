Gi vs No-Gi: What's Actually Different?

Both are genuine Brazilian Jiu Jitsu. Neither one is the "real" version. Here's what actually changes between the two, and which to try first.

Written with Fabio Ferrari, Black Belt & Head Coach, Impulse BJJ

"Which one is real BJJ?" is one of the most common questions from someone new to the sport. The honest answer: both are. Gi and No-Gi are two rulesets for the same underlying grappling art, and most serious practitioners eventually train both.

What Gi training is

Gi classes are trained in the traditional kimono: a heavy cotton jacket, trousers, and a belt marking your rank. The Gi itself becomes part of the technique, since grips on the sleeves, collar and trousers open up chokes, controls and sweeps that don't exist without fabric to grab. It's the more traditional side of the sport, and typically slower paced, since a good grip can shut down an entire attack before it starts.

What No-Gi training is

No-Gi classes are trained in a rash guard and shorts or spats: no jacket, no fabric grips. Without the Gi to slow things down, No-Gi tends to move faster, with more scrambling and a heavier reliance on underhooks, overhooks and body control rather than cloth grips. It's also the ruleset closest to what you'll see in MMA, since fighters obviously aren't wearing a kimono in a cage. New to terms like these? Check our BJJ glossary.

The real differences (and what stays the same)

The fundamentals, position before submission, control before attack, don't change between the two. What changes is which tools are available to get there. A grip you'd use to control someone's collar in the Gi simply doesn't exist in No-Gi, so you learn to control the same position with an underhook or a different angle instead. Neither ruleset is a shortcut past the other; they sharpen different parts of the same skill set.

Which should you try first?

There's no wrong answer. If you're planning to compete in traditional BJJ, lean toward Gi early to build grip-based habits. If your main interest is MMA or self-defence without a jacket involved, No-Gi will feel more directly transferable. Most beginners at Impulse end up training both within their first few months simply because the timetable makes it easy to.

Quick answers

Do I need to buy a gi to start training?

No. Come to your first class in a t-shirt and shorts or joggers. If you decide to continue and want to try a Gi class, your coach will point you towards what to buy and where.

Is No-Gi easier than Gi for beginners?

Not necessarily easier, just different. No-Gi tends to be faster paced with more emphasis on scrambles, while Gi rewards patience and grip control. Most beginners get value from trying both rather than picking one too early.

Is No-Gi better preparation for MMA?

Generally yes, since there's no kimono to grip in a real fight or MMA bout, so No-Gi grappling transfers more directly. That said, the control principles learned in the Gi still carry over.
