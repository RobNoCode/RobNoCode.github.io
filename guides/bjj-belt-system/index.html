The BJJ Belt Order Explained

Every belt from white to black, what the stripes mean, how long each stage genuinely takes, and how the kids system differs, written with our own head coach.

Written with Fabio Ferrari, Black Belt & Head Coach, Impulse BJJ

Brazilian Jiu Jitsu ranks you slower than almost any other martial art, and that's deliberate. A belt in BJJ is earned by demonstrating you can actually use what it represents under live resistance, not by attending a set number of classes or passing a one-off test.

The adult belt order

Adults progress through five main belts. Each one, except black, can carry up to four stripes marking progress within that rank before the next promotion.

What the stripes mean

Stripes (sometimes called grades or grau) are the small marks added to the end of a belt between full promotions. A coach awards them individually as a student's technique, understanding and mat time develop, so two students at the same belt with a different number of stripes are simply at different points within that same rank. Most belts carry up to four stripes before the next colour. More unfamiliar terms like this are covered in our BJJ glossary.

How long each belt actually takes

There's no fixed schedule, and be wary of anyone who gives you an exact number. As a rough, commonly cited range across the BJJ community: many consistent practitioners take somewhere around one to two years to reach blue belt, several more years through purple and brown, and often somewhere in the region of 8 to 12 years in total to reach black. Training frequency, competition experience, body type and simple luck with injuries all move that number significantly. Treat it as a broad shape, not a promise.

Black belt degrees don't stop at black

Black belt isn't the end of the ladder, it's a new one. Degrees (Dans) continue to be awarded, typically years apart, and the belt itself changes to mark the later ones:

DegreeBelt
Black beltBlack
1st to 6th DanBlack, with degree bars on the belt
7th DanRed & Black
8th DanRed & White (Coral)
9th DanRed

In practice, almost every black belt you'll ever train under sits somewhere in the 1st to 6th Dan range. Red and coral belts are reserved for a small number of the most senior figures in the sport worldwide, typically after several decades of teaching and contribution.

The kids belt system

Kids grading works differently to the adult system, partly because the same belt shouldn't mean the same thing for a 6 year old as it does for a 30 year old. Younger students typically start at white belt, then progress through grey, yellow, orange and green belts, often with their own sub-stripes, before becoming eligible for an adult belt in their mid-teens. The exact structure varies a little between academies and grading organisations, so ask your coach directly if you want your child's specific pathway explained.

At Impulse, our kids programme is grouped by age rather than belt alone, Tots for 4 to 5 year olds, Kids 1 for 6 to 8, and Kids 2 for 9 and up, so the grading conversation happens alongside age-appropriate coaching, not instead of it.

Quick answers

How many BJJ belts are there?

Adults progress through five main belts: white, blue, purple, brown and black. Each belt (except black) carries up to four stripes marking progress within that rank. Black belt itself has further degrees awarded over decades.

How long does it take to get a black belt in BJJ?

There's no fixed timeline, but many practitioners training consistently take somewhere in the region of 8 to 12 years to reach black belt. Pace depends heavily on training frequency, competition experience and the individual, so treat any specific number as a rough guide rather than a guarantee.

What do the stripes on a BJJ belt mean?

Stripes (also called grades) mark progress within a belt rank, awarded by a coach as a student's technique and understanding develop. Most belts carry up to four stripes before promotion to the next belt colour.

What belt colours do kids use in BJJ?

Kids belts differ from the adult system. Younger students typically progress through grey, yellow, orange and green belts before becoming eligible for an adult belt in their mid-teens, with the exact structure varying slightly between academies and grading bodies.
