Glossary

BJJ Terms Explained, in Plain English

Your first few classes come with a lot of new words. Here's what the ones you'll hear most actually mean, no jargon explaining jargon.

On this page

  1. Positions
  2. Submissions
  3. Techniques
  4. Concepts
  5. General terms

POSITIONS

Guard

A position where you're on your back using your legs to control your opponent's body and stop them from getting past.

Closed Guard

Guard with your legs locked around your opponent's waist, ankles crossed, keeping them tight against you.

Open Guard

Any guard where your legs aren't locked around your opponent, usually giving more mobility to sweep or attack.

Half Guard

A guard where you trap one of your opponent's legs between your own, controlling half of their base.

Mount

A dominant top position, sitting on your opponent's torso with a knee on each side.

Side Control

A top position across your opponent's body, chest to chest, controlling them from the side rather than straddling them.

Back Control

Attaching to your opponent's back with hooks in. Generally considered the most dominant position in BJJ.

SUBMISSIONS

Armbar

A submission that hyperextends the elbow, using your hips and legs for leverage against a straightened arm.

Triangle Choke

A choke where your legs form a figure-four around your opponent's neck and one of their arms.

Rear Naked Choke

A blood choke applied from back control, wrapping an arm around the neck.

Guillotine Choke

A front-facing choke where you wrap your opponent's neck under your arm, usually as they shoot in for a takedown.

Kimura

A shoulder lock, controlling a bent arm behind your opponent's back using a figure-four grip.

Americana

A shoulder lock applied from mount or side control, rotating the arm outward at an unnatural angle.

TECHNIQUES

Sweep

A technique that reverses top and bottom position, usually taking you from your back to on top.

Pass

Getting past your opponent's guard into a more dominant position, like side control or mount.

Takedown

Bringing a standing opponent to the ground under control.

Shrimp

A hip-escape movement used to create space when someone's pressuring or attacking you from the top.

Bridge

Arching your hips and back off the mat, usually to unbalance someone on top of you or escape a pin.

CONCEPTS

Base

Your balance and stability in a position, how hard you are to knock over or sweep.

Posture

Keeping your spine upright and head up in a position, which protects you from chokes and attacks.

Pressure

Using your body weight to make a position uncomfortable and restrict your opponent's breathing and movement.

Frames

Using your arms or legs as a rigid barrier to create space and stop someone closing the distance on you.

Hooks

Using your feet or legs to control your opponent's hips or legs, especially from back control.

Grips

Where and how you hold your opponent, whether on the Gi fabric or directly on the body in No-Gi.

GENERAL TERMS

Gi

The traditional BJJ uniform: a heavy cotton jacket, trousers and belt, used for grips as part of the technique. See our Gi vs No-Gi guide.

No-Gi

Training without the kimono, usually in a rash guard and shorts, with no fabric grips available.

Rolling

Live sparring, where two students apply techniques against each other at real resistance rather than cooperatively.

Drilling

Repeating a technique with a cooperative partner to build muscle memory before trying it live.

Tap

Signalling submission by tapping your opponent or the mat, ending the exchange immediately and safely.

Oss

A Japanese term used in many gyms as a sign of respect, acknowledgement or agreement.

Grau

Portuguese for "degree," used for the stripes marking progress within a belt. See our belt system guide.
